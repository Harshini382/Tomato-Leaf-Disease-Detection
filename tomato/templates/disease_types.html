Tomato___Bacterial_spot

Bacterial spot is a bacterial disease caused by Xanthomonas vesicatoria. It affects tomatoes, peppers, and other solanaceous plants, causing significant crop losses.

Symptoms:

  • Dark, water-soaked lesions on leaves
  • Small, raised spots with yellow halos
  • Fruit with raised, scab-like spots
  • Leaf drop and plant weakening

Treatment:

  • Use copper-based bactericides
  • Remove and destroy infected plants
  • Use disease-free seeds and transplants
  • Improve air circulation
🍂

Tomato___Early_blight

Early blight is a common fungal disease caused by Alternaria solani. It is one of the most widespread tomato diseases and can cause significant yield losses.

Symptoms:

  • Dark brown spots with concentric rings
  • Lower leaves affected first
  • Yellowing and drying of leaves
  • Fruit rot at the stem end

Treatment:

  • Apply fungicides (chlorothalonil, mancozeb)
  • Remove infected plant debris
  • Use resistant varieties
  • Mulch around plants
🌧️

Tomato___Late_blight

Late blight is a devastating fungal-like disease caused by Phytophthora infestans. Famous for causing the Irish Potato Famine, it can destroy entire crops in favorable conditions.

Symptoms:

  • Water-soaked, grayish-green spots
  • White fuzzy mold on leaf undersides
  • Rapidly spreading lesions
  • Plant collapse in humid conditions

Treatment:

  • Apply preventive fungicides
  • Remove infected plants immediately
  • Ensure good air circulation
  • Avoid overhead watering
🍄

Tomato___Leaf_Mold

Leaf mold is a fungal disease caused by Passalora fulva. It thrives in warm, humid conditions and primarily affects greenhouse tomatoes.

Symptoms:

  • Yellow spots on upper leaf surfaces
  • Olive-green to brown fuzzy mold below
  • Leaf curling and drying
  • Reduced fruit quality

Treatment:

  • Improve ventilation
  • Reduce humidity
  • Apply sulfur-based fungicides
  • Use resistant varieties
🔴

Tomato___Septoria_leaf_spot

Septoria leaf spot is caused by the fungus Septoria lycopersici. It is one of the most common and damaging tomato diseases, especially in wet conditions.

Symptoms:

  • Small dark spots with light centers
  • Multiple spots on leaves
  • Leaf drop from the bottom up
  • Stem and fruit lesions

Treatment:

  • Apply fungicides weekly
  • Remove lower infected leaves
  • Avoid overhead irrigation
  • Rotate crops
🕷️

Tomato___Spider_mites Two-spotted_spider_mite

Two-spotted spider mites (Tetranychus urticae) are arachnids that feed on plant cells. They are a common pest in hot, dry conditions and can cause significant damage.

Symptoms:

  • Stippled, yellow leaves
  • Fine webbing on leaves
  • Bronzed or dried leaves
  • Reduced plant vigor

Treatment:

  • Use insecticidal soap or oils
  • Introduce predatory mites
  • Keep plants well-watered
  • Increase humidity
🎯

Tomato___Target_Spot

Target spot is caused by the fungus Corynespora cassiicola. It affects many crops and can cause leaf spots, stem lesions, and fruit rot in tomatoes.

Symptoms:

  • Brown lesions with concentric rings
  • Spots that may merge
  • Leaf yellowing and drop
  • Sunken spots on fruit

Treatment:

  • Apply appropriate fungicides
  • Remove infected debris
  • Improve plant spacing
  • Use disease-free seeds
🦟

Tomato___Tomato_mosaic_virus

Tomato mosaic virus is a highly contagious viral disease that affects many solanaceous plants. It can survive in dried plant debris and tobacco products for years.

Symptoms:

  • Mottled light and dark green leaves
  • Leaf curling and distortion
  • Stunted plant growth
  • Yellowing of fruit

Treatment:

  • No cure - remove infected plants
  • Use resistant varieties
  • Disinfect tools and hands
  • Control insect vectors
🟡

Tomato___Tomato_Yellow_Leaf_Curl_Virus

TYLCV is a begomovirus transmitted by whiteflies. It is one of the most damaging diseases of tomato worldwide, causing severe economic losses.

Symptoms:

  • Upward leaf curling
  • Yellow leaf margins
  • Stunted plant growth
  • Reduced fruit set

Treatment:

  • Use resistant varieties
  • Control whitefly populations
  • Remove infected plants
  • Use reflective mulches
✅

Tomato___healthy

A healthy tomato leaf shows no signs of disease or pest damage. The leaf should be green, vibrant, and free of spots, discoloration, or abnormalities.

Characteristics:

  • Uniform green color
  • No spots or lesions
  • Strong, upright stems
  • Normal leaf development

Prevention Tips:

  • Maintain proper watering schedule
  • Provide adequate nutrition
  • Monitor plants regularly
  • Practice good garden hygiene
